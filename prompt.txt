Use a Connection Desert Index (CDI). The goal is not to predict “who is lonely,” but to identify neighbourhoods where the built environment + demographics + access to activities make social connection harder.

Your unit should be buurt or wijk level for Rotterdam, Den Haag, and Amsterdam.

Core idea

For each neighbourhood:

Connection Desert Index = Vulnerability Risk + Access Barriers − Social Infrastructure

Higher score = more likely to be a “connection desert.”

CDI_i = 0.4V_i + 0.35A_i - 0.25S_i

Where:

V_i = demographic vulnerability score
A_i = accessibility barrier score
S_i = social infrastructure score
i = neighbourhood

A higher CDI_i means the neighbourhood should be prioritized.

Step 1: Normalize every variable

Because your variables have different units, convert everything to a 0–100 score.

Use min-max normalization:

X'_i = 100 × (X_i - min(X)) / (max(X) - min(X))

Example: if elderly share ranges from 5% to 30%, a neighbourhood with 20% elderly residents gets:

100 × (20 - 5) / (30 - 5) = 60

So the elderly-risk score is 60/100.

For “good” variables, like number of libraries or cafés, reverse the interpretation when calculating risk. More social infrastructure should reduce the final CDI.

Step 2: Build the vulnerability score

This measures whether the population has characteristics associated with higher risk of social isolation.

A simple formula:

V_i = 0.35E_i + 0.30H_i + 0.20T_i + 0.15M_i

Where:

E_i = share of residents aged 65+
H_i = share of single-person households
T_i = housing turnover / recent movers
M_i = migrant or non-Dutch-background share, used carefully as a language/accessibility barrier, not as “risk by ethnicity”

For a student-heavy version, add student density:

V_i = 0.25E_i + 0.25H_i + 0.20T_i + 0.15M_i + 0.15ST_i

Where ST_i is student density. This helps detect areas with international students or transient student populations.

Step 3: Build the accessibility barrier score

This captures how difficult it is to reach places where connection can happen.

A_i = 0.35PT_i + 0.30W_i + 0.20D_i + 0.15R_i

Where:

PT_i = poor public transport access
W_i = poor walkability
D_i = average distance to nearest community centre/library/cultural space
R_i = rain/cold/seasonality penalty, especially relevant if outdoor social infrastructure is high but indoor places are low

For transport access, calculate something like:

PT_i = 100 - normalized number of transit stops within 500m

So if an area has many nearby stops, the barrier score is low.

For distance:

D_i = normalized average distance to nearest social facility

Longer distance = higher barrier.

Step 4: Build the social infrastructure score

This measures how many low-pressure connection opportunities exist nearby.

S_i = 0.30C_i + 0.25L_i + 0.20P_i + 0.15B_i + 0.10EV_i

Where:

C_i = cafés, community centres, sports clubs, cultural venues
L_i = libraries and municipal/community facilities
P_i = parks and green spaces
B_i = benches and informal public sitting areas
EV_i = number of relevant events nearby

Important: weight free or low-cost spaces more than commercial spaces. A café is useful, but a library or community centre is more inclusive.

So you can create weighted infrastructure points:

S_i = 3(community centres) + 3(libraries) + 2(parks) + 1(cafés) + 1(benches) + 2(free events)

Then normalize the final S_i to 0–100.

Final formula for your prototype

Use this:

CDI_i = 0.40V_i + 0.35A_i + 0.25(100 - S_i)

This version is easier to explain because all three components increase loneliness risk:

high vulnerability increases CDI
poor accessibility increases CDI
lack of social infrastructure increases CDI

So:

100 - S_i

means “social infrastructure shortage.”

Example interpretation

Suppose a neighbourhood has:

Vulnerability score = 75
Accessibility barrier score = 60
Social infrastructure score = 30

Then:

CDI = 0.40(75) + 0.35(60) + 0.25(100 - 30)

CDI = 30 + 21 + 17.5 = 68.5

So that neighbourhood gets a Connection Desert Index of 68.5/100, meaning it is a high-priority area.

Classification

Use simple categories for the map:

CDI score	Label	Colour
0–30	Low connection risk	Green
30–50	Moderate risk	Yellow
50–70	High risk	Orange
70–100	Severe connection desert	Red

This makes the output easy for judges and municipalities to understand.

Add a “why this area?” explanation

For each neighbourhood, your AI should produce an explanation like:

“This neighbourhood scores high because it has a high share of single-person households and elderly residents, weak public transport access, and few free indoor meeting spaces within 15 minutes. Recommended interventions: weekly library coffee table, mobile community van, and volunteer walking group.”

This is what makes the model feel intelligent, not just mathematical.
